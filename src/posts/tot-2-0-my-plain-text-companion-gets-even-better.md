<p>I love <a href="https://tot.rocks/">Tot</a>. I love how it treats plain text as a first-class citizen, and I use it constantly alongside Apple&#8217;s Notes app. The sync is rock solid, and it&#8217;s like a Swiss Army knife for quick text capture.</p>



<p>I use Tot on both iPhone and Mac. On iPhone, I have a Control Center shortcut for Quick Note in the Notes app to capture text snippets for later use. I fire up Tot on Mac and iPhone to jot down snippets of information like meeting notes or phone numbers during calls. I also depend on the helpful stats display like &#8220;31 lines • 103 words • 646 characters&#8221; to keep track of my content.</p>



<p>I got a chance to test <a href="https://tot.rocks/history">Tot 2.0</a> after <a href="https://mastodon.social/@gedeonm/114875208985310659">reaching out</a> to the nice folks at <a href="https://iconfactory.com/">Icon Factory</a>, and this new update brings some really useful improvements.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What&#8217;s new in 2.0</h2>



<p>One of the most helpful additions is the timestamp showing when each dot was last updated. This seemingly small feature eliminates a lot of confusion about which notes are current.</p>



<p>The new version simplifies many features that were hidden in previous versions. While I don&#8217;t personally use the Smart Bullets functionality, I&#8217;m excited about the new text dividers feature, which offers eight different styles accessible through the symbol picker.</p>



<p>The app continues to stay out of my way. I can still hit the escape key to quickly dismiss it. While customizable dot colors would have been a nice touch, I appreciate that the app now shows dot numbers.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Plain text, always</h2>



<p>I always use Tot in plain text mode. If I need formatting, there&#8217;s the Notes app for that. I stick with the Menlo font and keep things simple. The automatic backups are excellent, and there&#8217;s now a proper history feature. I used to struggle with losing text occasionally, so this improvement gives me peace of mind.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">iPhone experience</h2>



<p>On iPhone, I wish the &#8220;Compact Dots&#8221; feature was available as a keyboard shortcut (this feature moves full dots to the left to eliminate empty gaps, though you can&#8217;t undo this action). I also use asterisks to make lists constantly, and having that symbol more accessible on the keyboard would be helpful.</p>



<p>I tried Sindre Sorhus&#8217;s barebones <a href="https://sindresorhus.com/scratchpad">Scratchpad</a> app, but the iCloud sync still seems unreliable compared to Tot&#8217;s rock-solid syncing.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Interface improvements</h2>



<p>The redesigned settings screen is much cleaner and more discoverable. The new undo/redo buttons in the status bar of the iPhone app are handy. I&#8217;d love to see the status bar become more configurable in the future, since I don&#8217;t use the formatting, indentation, or Smart Bullets features.</p>



<p>Tot 2.0 refines an already excellent app. The Icon Factory team has focused on the right improvements: better organization, clearer interfaces, and rock-solid reliability. For anyone who needs quick, reliable text capture across devices, Tot remains an essential tool.</p>



<p>The app doesn&#8217;t try to be everything to everyone, and that&#8217;s exactly why it works so well. Sometimes the best tools are the ones that do one thing exceptionally well, and Tot continues to excel at being my tiny text companion.</p>
